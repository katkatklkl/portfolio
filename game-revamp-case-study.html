Game UI Revamp

A UI/UX case study on redesigning the home screen and character customization experience for a game that had outgrown its original design.

Role 2D Game Artist · User Testing · UX
Tools Adobe Suite · Miro · FigJam
Focus Starting Screen · Character Customization
Platform iOS & Android
Type Game UX · Redesign
Overview

My Role at EA

Outside of my 2D design work at EA, I had the opportunity to contribute to a broader UI/UX revamp the team was undertaking. The game was 8 years old and had evolved significantly since, with a much larger catalogue of items, clothing, and features that the original UI was never designed to support. My involvement spanned the brainstorming, testing, and design phases, with a focus on reworking the starting screen and character customization screen. I was also working on redesigning the tutorial scene, though that part of the project was cancelled before it shipped.

The Problem

Players Were Leaving Early

Data reports showed low retention rates, a significant number of players were quitting or deleting the game after only the first few screens or a few minutes of gameplay. The core goal of the revamp was to address this and bring those retention numbers up.

The early drop-off pointed to the first impression experience as a key area of concern. If players weren't sticking around past the opening screens (the starting screen and character customization experience which is the first things they interact with), we needed to do a much better job of drawing them in. To understand what wasn't working, team members tested the existing screens and documented our observations.

Starting Screen — Key Findings

01

Branding Needed a Refresh

The game's identity is built around Luxury, Glamour, and High Fashion, but the starting screen wasn't communicating any of that. As the player's very first impression of the game, it needed to reflect the brand much more clearly and confidently.

02

Visual Noise and Clutter

The background environment behind the UI was too busy, with text and visual elements competing for attention rather than supporting it. This made it harder for players to focus on the key actions and UI buttons they actually needed to interact with.

03

Outdated Visual Style

The original design hadn't been meaningfully revisited since the game launched. Nearly a decade later, the visual language, typography, color, and layout felt out of step with where the game and its player base had grown.

Character Customization Screen — Key Findings

01

Content Had Outgrown the Structure

The character customization screen was built for a much smaller inventory. Years of new items and clothing options made it increasingly hard to browse, and the layout couldn't accommodate the volume without feeling cluttered and overwhelming.

02

The UI Lacked Visual Cohesion

Category icons were white, button borders were purple, the stars currency was gold, energy was blue, and cash was green. There was no unifying visual language holding it all together, and the result felt scattered and inconsistent rather than intentional.

03

Navigation Relied Too Heavily on Icons Alone

While the category icons were recognisable for the original item types, years of new additions meant many items no longer fit neatly under a single icon. Without category names or tooltips to support the icons, players had no clear way to tell where something would be found, making browsing more confusing than it needed to be.

Results

The Redesign

01

Gold as the Design Foundation

The team all loved the golden "Start" button from the original design, so we carried it forward into the redesign. From there, we built an entire visual language around it, using gold and metallic sheen as the anchoring aesthetic to communicate the luxury and glamour the brand had always stood for but never fully expressed visually.

02

A Cleaner, More Focused Screen

The cluttered background environment was replaced with a clean, minimal backdrop that lets the character and UI take center stage. Removing the visual noise meant players could immediately focus on the key actions in front of them rather than competing with everything around it.

03

Typography That Feels Like the Brand

We replaced the outdated typefaces with fonts that draw on the visual language of high-end luxury brands, the kind you'd see on a fashion house billboard or a couture label. The new typography instantly elevates the tone and signals to players exactly what kind of world they're stepping into.

01

A Unified Icon Set

All category icons were redesigned using the same gold metallic palette introduced on the home screen. The new icons are cleaner, more readable, and visually consistent with one another, bringing a cohesion to the UI that the original design never had.

02

Named Categories for Clearer Navigation

Icon labels were added to every category, so players no longer have to guess where something lives. The combination of a redesigned icon and a clear name makes browsing significantly faster and less frustrating, especially as the item catalogue continues to grow.

03

Familiar Layout, Refreshed Look

The three-per-row grid structure that longtime players know was kept intentionally intact. The goal was to modernise the visual experience and improve navigation without disorienting the existing player base. The layout stays the same, everything else gets elevated.
